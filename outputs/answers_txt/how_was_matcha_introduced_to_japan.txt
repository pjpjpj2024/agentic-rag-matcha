USER QUERY
How was matcha introduced to Japan?

FINAL ANSWER
Matcha was introduced to Japan in the **twelfth century** through China, brought by a Zen monk who had studied abroad and returned with tea seeds. This monk established a devotional approach to tea preparation, treating the process as a spiritual practice. During the **Kamakura period** (roughly a century later), Zen monks began using powdered tea specifically to stay alert during long meditation sessions, integrating it into monastic rituals. By the **sixteenth century**, a tea master under a powerful military ruler formalized the practice into a distinct aesthetic style emphasizing **restraint, simplicity, and quiet**, which remains influential in Japan today.  

The custom of ritual tea drinking, originating in China, was first practiced in Japan by Zen monks for meditation. Over time, it became tied to Zen rituals, including ceremonies honoring the tradition's first patriarch. By the **fifteenth century**, the practice expanded beyond religious contexts into social gatherings where art, calligraphy, and tea utensils were discussed, though its roots in Zen discipline and mindfulness remained central.
